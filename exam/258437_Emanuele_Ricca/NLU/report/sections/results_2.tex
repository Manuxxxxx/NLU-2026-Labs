\section{Results and Evaluation}
\label{sec:results_2}

Models are evaluated on the ATIS dataset using sentence-level Intent Classification Accuracy and CoNLL-style token-level Slot F1 metrics. Table \ref{tab:2a_results} presents the empirical scaling metrics across the selected 8 representative design configurations evaluated from scratch, while Table \ref{tab:2b_results} details the pre-trained transfer fine-tuning benchmarks.

\begin{table}[H]

\label{tab:2a_results}
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{%
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
\textbf{Incremental Modification Variant} & \textbf{Intent Acc.} & \textbf{Slot F1} \\ \hline
Provided Baseline ($d_{\text{model}}\!=\!20, L\!=\!1, H\!=\!1, \text{FF}_{\text{dim}}\!=\!20$) & 0.8847 & 0.8093 \\

The course baseline yields an Intent Accuracy of 0.8847 and a Slot F1 score of 0.8093. Expanding the basic hidden dimensions directly to an intermediate ($d_{\text{model}}=64$) and higher-tier capacity ($d_{\text{model}}=128, \text{FF}_{\text{dim}}=192$) drives immediate improvements, yielding a clean score bump across both prediction tasks. This underscores that raw hidden layer width represents the primary driver of joint multi-task tracking stability.

Conversely, changing specialized parameters in isolation yields fluctuating outcomes. Moving block depth to $L=4$ or multi-head tracking counts to $H=2$ in isolation causes metrics to drop slightly due to overfitting configurations on the compact sample layout of the ATIS data splits. Introducing an intermediate balanced depth layout ($L=2, d_{\text{model}}=64$) improves performance compared to isolated deeper frameworks, demonstrating that parameter balance is essential. Integrating an optimized 0.1 output dropout layer on top of the heavily parameterized deep-scaled model balances the parameter tracking, achieving peak scratch performance at 0.9395 Intent Accuracy and 0.8906 Slot F1.

Fine-tuning pre-trained foundation backbones significantly outperforms all models built from scratch[cite: 2, 5, 8, 11]. The basic trial using \texttt{GPT2-small} outpaces the custom networks within 3 epochs[cite: 8]. Fully fine-tuning the base \texttt{GPT2} network increases performance to 0.9586 Intent Accuracy and an 0.8949 Slot F1 score[cite: 11, 12]. 

The bidirectional \texttt{BERT-base-uncased} model records the highest overall metrics, achieving a peak Intent Accuracy of 0.9765 and an optimal Slot F1 of 0.9332[cite: 5, 6]. BERT consistently outpaces GPT2 on both targets[cite: 2, 5, 8, 11]. This variance is strictly structural: BERT’s unconstrained bidirectional attention mask extracts context from both sequence directions simultaneously, matching sequence-tagging token dynamics. In contrast, GPT2 is bounded by causal left-to-right attention fields that prevent downstream token representations from informing earlier sequence slot states.

As displayed in the comparative tracking of Figure \ref{fig:scratch_vs_pretrained_metrics}, plotting scratch models directly alongside pre-trained configurations visually maps the paradigm shift enabled by transfer learning. In Figure \ref{fig:intent_acc_2a_2b}, the fine-tuned systems bypass the initial training convergence cycles entirely, initializing immediately at an accuracy baseline that outperforms custom scratch networks even after thousands of optimization updates. This divergence is reinforced by token-level sequence evaluations in Figure \ref{fig:slot_f1_2a_2b}. The pre-trained context representations allow \texttt{BERT-base} to establish a clear, dominant upper bound on slot detection, reaching stable tracking milestones that scratch configurations cannot emulate due to overfitting layout limitations.
